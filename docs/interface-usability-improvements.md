# Interface usability improvements

This pass makes the platform easier to enter and navigate for mathematicians who are new to it. The interface keeps the current proof and next useful action in view, while putting less frequent controls and technical record details behind clearly named disclosures.

## Important changes

- The home page distinguishes starting a problem, reopening an exported proof file, and resuming a saved proof. The new-problem flow explains the mathematical information needed first and reveals optional setup when needed.
- The proof workspace gives a short selection cue and keeps the formal/natural-language switch, export, and discovery-tree access visible. Secondary tools and advanced proof actions remain available under compact controls.
- Suggestion cards focus on the proposed step, why it is offered, and its mathematical effect. Each card still identifies a library result or reusable move at a glance; matching details, provenance, and parameters remain inspectable. When no suggestion applies, the panel points to ways to continue exploring, including move authoring.
- The library drawer explains what it contains and how to preview a result by dragging it onto a statement.
- The history views explain the purpose of the tree, playback, and pruned proof in ordinary language. Tree states are named for browsing; internal IDs, operation names, and playback ordering details are available on demand.

The changes are presentational. Proof commands, stored snapshots, suggestion order, and the distinction between proven steps and explicit assumptions retain their existing semantics.
